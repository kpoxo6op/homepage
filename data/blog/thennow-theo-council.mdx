## How the site started

ChatGPT came out at the end of 2022, and GPT-4 followed in March 2023. Almost as soon as ChatGPT landed in my hands, I had an idea to make a website, [thennow.nz](https://thennow.nz). The [first commit](https://github.com/kpoxo6op/thennow/commit/c0049bd9820aa13576428988e39ad1afeb877ad2) is from May 2023. I did not understand anything about React development, and I still don't, so I built the whole site by copy-pasting with ChatGPT. I managed to take a few photos and drive around a bit, but then I got very busy and forgot the site existed.

I came back to it now and then, took a few more photos and played with artificial intelligence a little to measure how much easier it had become. It had become much easier. For example, I no longer had to copy anything into a chat and back. Everything worked on the computer from the terminal.

## Two tricks this week

Since I am an autist obsessed with artificial intelligence, x.com, Telegram and the rest of the internet are, for me, one continuous stream of news and content about AI. Lately I keep seeing skills and methods for improving UX and UI. This week alone I came across two. I tried them today on my website.

One of them was, I think, the [Dennison skill](https://github.com/dennisonbertram/developer-skill-pack). I asked ChatGPT to set up two sessions in Claude Code. One session worked in vanilla Claude Code, and the second used Dennison’s skills.

## What I asked GPT to do

Again, I did it very simply. I opened the same chat with GPT that I had been using before. I told it: launch T3 and run an experiment there. Redo the homepage the way Theo recommends in [his post on x.com](https://x.com/theo/status/2118465321032106235). Then I told it to do the same for the Stories section. You can see the result in the videos. Theo’s method is also published as a [skill](https://github.com/t3dotgg/tstack/blob/1085050f3baed1ac724caa4a8166145fb2ac88af/skills/council/SKILL.md).

I did not burn as many tokens as Theo might recommend. My model chose for itself how much to spend on iterations. I think I had 4 or 5 experiments for the homepage and for the stories, while in screenshots and reviews other developers wrote that they did 10 experiments, if I remember correctly, 10 iterations. And maybe they used more powerful models. I used only Claude Opus 5.5 and GPT-6.1 Sol. I don’t even know at what power. The agent decided that for me too, naturally.

What changed compared with the old version:

- The homepage opens on one big then/now photo you drag, instead of a map.
- The map is now a quiet option; the page scrolls through every story as paired photos.
- Every page shares one dark look, one typeface and one wordmark.
- A story shows its years, a "drag the line" hint, the full history with its source, and the original and modern photos on their own.
- The end of a story offers the next one and the nearest others, with distances.

I’m ready to pray to [tstack](https://github.com/t3dotgg/tstack) for this alone: it figured out by itself that inside a story it should show the original photograph and the modern shot separately, each on its own.

## Why I am writing this

I am writing this post because it blew my mind. I almost cried from an overflow of feelings. The jump in UX and UI quality was just impossible. For the effort spent versus the result, I have never done anything like it in my life. It feels like magic. I did not suffer, I did not write, I did not slave away. I just sent a screenshot of Theo’s post on x.com and got an explosive jump in UX and UI quality, without even explaining what exactly I want. I just said: make it pretty and send me the results.

I can’t even imagine what will happen if I do really deep research, understand what I actually want from the site and give the agents a direction. I’m afraid I’ll just lose sleep for a week. I don’t think I’ll survive it, because I have already lost sleep over what just happened. I’m dictating this post at half past midnight because I can’t sleep. There is one more reason I can’t sleep, but I can’t tell you about it. It’s my little secret. And it has nothing to do with artificial intelligence, thank God.

Anyway, a chaotic post, but I want it chaotic, so it comes from the heart. And I beg you: if you have pet projects at work or at home, or any interface with UX and UI, install T3 at any cost and spend 10 iterations improving some part of the UX and UI that is dear to you. I bet you will either shed a tear or have some other strong emotions, and in short, you will be very surprised by the result.

See you next time. I’ll try to go to sleep. And I’ll spend the last half hour editing this post for my website and for a LinkedIn post.
